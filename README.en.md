# Delta Electronics: Geopolitical Risk, Global Strategy and Knowledge Integration

[繁體中文](README.md) · [Case analysis](docs/case-study.zh-TW.md) · [Theory to practice](docs/theory-to-practice.zh-TW.md) · [Execution proposal](docs/strategy-playbook.zh-TW.md) · [Contribution statement](docs/contribution.md)

**How can a multinational diversify production while preserving technical advantage and keeping coordination costs under control?**

This portfolio develops a Delta Electronics case from a 2025 Business Policy and Strategy group project at National Cheng Kung University and Eason Lee’s individual literature presentation. It connects international business, ESG as a nonmarket strategy, knowledge complementarity and dynamic capabilities.

![Analytical structure and strategic choices](assets/strategy-map.svg)

## Strategic argument

Geographic diversification creates options, but those options require local execution and cross-border knowledge integration. Local teams need enough discretion to respond to customers and institutional demands. Shared technical knowledge, interfaces and transfer routines allow the wider organization to benefit from what each location learns.

The resulting recommendation is to evaluate production choices by product, supply route and customer market; retain alternative capacity where justified; and pair local decision rights with a structured process for finding, testing and transferring knowledge. ESG disclosure can support trust and reduce information asymmetry, but revenue growth alone does not identify its financial effect.

## Three connected decisions

| Decision | Analytical lens | Proposed action and trade-off |
|---|---|---|
| How much of the value chain should move? | Specificity and destructiveness of institutional pressures; multinational agency | Use a contingent location strategy with review triggers. Redundancy improves flexibility but adds cost and management demands. |
| What should ESG accomplish? | Nonmarket strategy, legitimacy and investor information | Report site-level performance and remediation with traceable evidence. Assess outcomes separately from company-wide sales growth. |
| How should dispersed technical assets create value? | Knowledge-based view and dynamic capabilities | Connect local sensing to funded cross-unit trials and documented transfer. Autonomy requires incentives and coordination to avoid isolated knowledge. |

## Research interpretation

The individual presentation reviews Aliyev and Lee’s *Knowledge-Based Assets in Business Groups: A Dynamic Capabilities View of Complementarity and Rents* ([DOI](https://doi.org/10.1177/01492063251323849)). The authors study 524 Japanese keiretsu affiliates during 1985–2015 using patent and firm data. The research and empirical analysis belong to those authors.

The public portfolio checks selected coefficients against Table 2 of the supplied article, identifying discrepancies in slide 7 of the historical presentation. It also distinguishes the hypothesis-specific models from the full model: in the vertical sample’s full model, the sensing interaction has p = .057. These findings should not be converted into a claim that every effect is significant in every specification, or into a forecast of Delta’s return on sales. See the [research review](docs/research-review.zh-TW.md) and [audited aggregate data](data/research-audit.json).

The 2024 company annual report also describes strong AI data-center demand alongside weaker application markets. This provides a concrete alternative explanation that an ESG–revenue argument must address. [Annual report, PDF p.5](https://filecenter.deltaww.com/IR/download/annual_report/2024annual.pdf)
